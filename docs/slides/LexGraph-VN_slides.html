Motivation

Correct article, wrong answer

"How much is the fine for running a red light on a motorbike?"

  • Vietnamese law changes constantly: laws are amended, decrees replaced, circulars repealed. The fine above changed in 2022 and again in 2025.
  • A retriever that returns a repealed article is confidently wrong, yet standard metrics count it as a hit.
  • Legal texts form a web: documents amend, replace, guide and cite each other.
31.3%

of the QA passages we linked to the national legal portal come from documents that are no longer in force.

Another 32.6% come from documents that are partly expired.

Problem and approach

Answer under the law in force at question time

Input: a question $q$ and a date $t_q$. Output: the articles in force at $t_q$, a short cited answer, and a notice if the rule has changed.

Our project as a web mining pipeline

Web crawling

Collect the legal portal's pages politely and keep them up to date.

Web content mining

Turn each HTML page into articles; extract references and amendments from the text.

Web structure mining

Build the link graph between documents; the links tell which law is still valid.

Retrieval and ranking

Search the mined corpus; rank by meaning and by validity at $t_q$.

RQ1 How often is the retrieved law outdated, and can link-derived validity fix it? · RQ2 Which link types matter? · RQ3 Can a small LLM (Qwen3-4B) cite the law and explain changes?

Which version of an article is in force?

amended by →

  • For a passage in document $D$, candidate successors are articles in documents that amend, replace or repeal $D$ (up to 2 hops).
  • Alignment classifier: a gradient-boosting model decides whether a new article is the successor of an old one, from text similarity, cross-encoder score, word overlap and link type; 2,359 training pairs labelled by an LLM judge; each old article keeps at most one successor.
  • Successor articles are chunked by clause and added to the corpus, so the current law can be retrieved.

System

Pipeline

Offline (web mining)

Crawl vbpl.vn→ Articles, references, amendments→ Link graph → validity intervals→ Version alignment→ Index: QA passages + successor articles

Online (question answering)

Query normalisation→BGE-M3 (fine-tuned) top-100→ Inject successors of stale candidates→ BGE-Reranker (fine-tuned) top-30→ Stale penalty at $t_q$→ Qwen3-4B (vLLM)→ Streamlit demo
crawlingcontent miningstructure miningretrieval · everything runs on our own server (2×H200), no external LLM API
Retrieval and rankingmethod

Time-aware re-ranking

$$s(q,d) = \underbrace{s_{\mathrm{CE}}(q,d)}_{\text{semantic}} - \gamma\,\underbrace{\mathbf{1}\left[d \text{ not in force at } t_q\right]}_{\text{stale penalty}}$$
  • Successor injection: the successors of stale candidates join the cross-encoder candidates, so the current version is found even when the retriever only sees the old one.
  • Query normalisation: everyday wording is mapped to legal wording first ("run a red light" → "fail to obey traffic light signals").
  • $\gamma$ tuned on validation by MRR@10 against the current gold ($\gamma = 2$).
  • New metric Stale@k: share of the top-$k$ results not in force at question time.
Answer generation · "law changed" notices

Grounded answers, verified change notices

  • Qwen3-4B on vLLM; context = most relevant clauses of articles in force, the old version of a changed article and its amendments.
  • Prompt rules: answer only from the given articles, cite each claim as [k], refuse when evidence is missing.
  • A notice is allowed only from a verified change fact: the clause asked about differs (fine amounts, or token Jaccard < 0.85) between the old article and its successor, and Qwen3-4B confirms the answer to this question differs.
  • After generation, unsupported notices are removed and missing ones added.

  • Semi-hard negatives: from the top-100, skip the first ranks and anything within 0.05 of the positive (avoids unlabeled true answers).
  • Bi-encoder: multiple-negatives ranking loss, 3 semi-hard negatives, 105,899 pairs, global batch 128, ~25 min.
  • Cross-encoder: binary cross-entropy, 1 positive + 7 negatives mined with the fine-tuned bi-encoder, 445,296 pairs, ~30 min.

Conclusion

What we learned, what comes next

Takeaways

  • Validity matters: 31% of linked gold passages are outdated; validity signals halve outdated results (Stale@10 0.234 → 0.125).
  • Links, not centrality: amends / replaces / repeals edges tell which article is current; PageRank did not help ranking.
  • Fine-tuning pays most: MRR@10 0.539 → 0.704 on test.
  • The 4B reader is the bottleneck, not retrieval.

Next steps

  • Old versions as hard negatives (temporal contrastive training).
  • Higher recall of version alignment.
  • Claim-level citation checking against the cited clause.
  • Multi-hop Law → Decree → Circular answers; user study.
